## 1. Purpose and scope

Loaded Dice is committed to providing a fair, consistent, and timely process for dealing with employee concerns. This procedure ensures that any problems, complaints, or grievances are addressed appropriately.

This policy applies to all employees of Loaded Dice.

Employees will not be treated unfavourably for raising a grievance in good faith, even if the complaint is not upheld.

## 2. Introduction

A grievance may arise when an employee has a concern or complaint relating to their employment. Examples include, but are not limited to:

**2.1** Work, working conditions, pay and benefits, or working hours.

**2.2** Discrimination on the grounds of race, sex, sexual orientation, religion or belief, disability, age, gender reassignment, marital status, or ethnic origin.

**2.3** Treatment by colleagues, including harassment and bullying.

**2.4** Health and safety concerns or breaches of statutory employment rights.

**2.5** Any other issue affecting their employment.

> Complaints regarding disciplinary action should be raised through the disciplinary appeal procedure, not this grievance procedure.

## 3. Informal procedure

**3.1** Where possible, employees should first raise their concern informally with their immediate manager. If the grievance involves the immediate manager, the employee should instead approach the next most senior manager.

**3.2** The manager will discuss the concern with the employee and attempt to resolve the matter promptly, normally within 10 working days.

**3.3** Both parties should keep a written record of any meeting, including what was discussed and any proposed action.

**3.4** If the matter cannot be resolved informally, the employee may raise a formal grievance.

## 4. Formal grievance procedure

### 4.1 Written statement

**4.1.1** The employee should submit a written statement outlining the nature of their grievance to their line manager without unreasonable delay.

**4.1.2** If the grievance concerns the line manager, the written statement should instead be sent to the Managing Director.

### 4.2 Grievance meeting

**4.2.1** The line manager will arrange a formal grievance meeting within 5 working days of receiving the statement, unless exceptional circumstances apply (in which case the employee will be informed in writing).

**4.2.2** The manager must be given reasonable time to review the grievance before the meeting takes place.

**4.2.3** A thorough investigation of the facts will be carried out before the meeting. Requests for anonymity or confidentiality will be considered where practicable.

**4.2.4** The employee has the right to be accompanied by a colleague, a certified trade union representative, or an official employed by a trade union. The companion may address the meeting, support the employee, and confer with them but may not answer questions on their behalf.

**4.2.5** Loaded Dice may refuse a companion if their presence could compromise the fairness or integrity of the process.

**4.2.6** The employee, manager, and any companion must make every effort to attend the meeting. If the employee fails to attend without good reason, the meeting may proceed in their absence.

**4.2.7** The employee should, where possible, explain how they believe the grievance could be resolved.

**4.2.8** If further investigation is required, the meeting may be adjourned and reconvened once the investigation is complete.

### 4.3 Outcome of meeting

**4.3.1** Following the meeting (and any investigation), the manager will provide a written outcome, normally within 5 working days, setting out any decision and action to be taken.

**4.3.2** The employee will also be informed of their right to appeal if they are dissatisfied with the outcome.

**4.3.3** Any agreed actions will be monitored and reviewed as appropriate to ensure effectiveness.

### 4.4 Appeal

**4.4.1** Employees have the right to appeal if they feel their grievance has not been satisfactorily resolved.

**4.4.2** An appeal must be submitted in writing, stating the grounds for appeal, within 5 working days of receiving the written outcome.

**4.4.3** A further meeting will be arranged within a reasonable timeframe. The employee will be informed in advance of the time and place.

**4.4.4** The appeal will be impartial and, wherever possible, chaired by a manager not previously involved in the case and of greater seniority.

**4.4.5** The appeal hearing is not a re-hearing of the original grievance, but a review of the specific grounds of dissatisfaction.

**4.4.6** The employee has the right to be accompanied at the appeal meeting.

**4.4.7** The outcome of the appeal will be confirmed in writing within 5 working days. This decision is final, and the grievance procedure is concluded.

## 5. Confidentiality

**5.1** Grievances will be handled with the highest level of confidentiality practicable.

**5.2** Records will be stored securely in line with data protection legislation.

**5.3** Copies of meeting notes will be provided to the employee, although certain information may be withheld to protect confidentiality (e.g. witness statements).

## 6. Special cases

**6.1** Where a grievance is raised during a disciplinary process, the disciplinary process may be temporarily suspended if the grievance is directly related to the case.

**6.2** This procedure does not apply to collective grievances.

**6.3** Separate procedures exist for concerns involving bullying, harassment, or whistleblowing, particularly where serious misconduct, legal breaches, or regulatory obligations are involved.
