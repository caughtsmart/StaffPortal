## 1. Introduction

Loaded Dice is committed to supporting employees in balancing their career and family responsibilities. We recognise the importance of enabling employees to support their partner following the birth of a child. This policy sets out employees' rights and responsibilities in relation to paternity leave. A separate policy applies for adoptive parents.

## 2. Aims of the policy

- To provide employees with the time they need to care for their new child and support their partner.
- To comply with all statutory obligations relating to paternity leave and pay, and, where applicable, to provide enhanced benefits as outlined in section 7.

## 3. Antenatal appointments

Employees are entitled to take unpaid time off to accompany a pregnant partner to up to two antenatal appointments, with a maximum of six and a half hours per appointment.

## 4. Paternity leave

### 4.1 Eligibility

Employees are entitled to up to two consecutive weeks (or one week, if preferred) of Paternity Leave if they:

- Expect to share responsibility for the child's upbringing; and
- Are the child's biological father or the partner of the child's mother; and
- Have at least 26 weeks' continuous employment with Loaded Dice by the end of the 15th week before the expected week of childbirth; and
- Provide Loaded Dice with a completed self-certificate confirming eligibility.

### 4.2 Start of leave

Paternity Leave may begin:

- On the date of the child's birth (whether earlier or later than expected); or
- On a chosen date within 56 days of the actual date of birth; or
- Within 56 days of the expected week of childbirth if the baby is born early.

Leave must be taken in a single block (one or two consecutive weeks). Only one period of Paternity Leave is available, regardless of whether more than one child is born from the same pregnancy.

### 4.3 Relationship with shared parental leave

Employees who wish to take both Paternity Leave and Shared Parental Leave must take their Paternity Leave first.

## 5. In the event of death of the baby

- If a stillbirth occurs after 24 weeks of pregnancy, the employee remains entitled to Paternity Leave.
- If a stillbirth occurs before 24 weeks, the employee may take sick leave or compassionate leave, at the discretion of their line manager or HR.
- If the baby is born alive but later dies, the employee remains entitled to Paternity Leave.

## 6. Procedure

Employees must notify their line manager or HR by the 15th week before the expected week of childbirth, unless this is not reasonably practicable. Notification should include:

- The expected week of childbirth;
- Whether they intend to take one or two weeks; and
- The intended start date of leave.

Employees may change their intended start date provided they give at least 28 days' notice in writing. HR will confirm the agreed arrangements in writing. Employees do not need to provide notice of their return date unless they wish to return earlier than planned.

## 7. Paternity pay

### 7.1 Statutory Paternity Pay (SPP)

Eligible employees will receive SPP for up to two weeks of Paternity Leave. SPP is paid at the statutory rate (set annually by HMRC) or 90% of average weekly earnings if this is lower.

Employees earning below the lower earnings limit for National Insurance will not qualify for SPP but may be eligible for other financial support via GOV.UK.

## 8. Rights during paternity leave

During Paternity Leave, employees:

- Remain employed under their contract, with all terms and conditions continuing, except for pay.
- Will be kept informed of work-related news, including invitations to meetings and social events, as appropriate.
- Will return to the same job on the same terms and conditions following Paternity Leave.

## 9. Shared parental leave

Employees may take both Paternity Leave and Shared Parental Leave, provided that Paternity Leave comes first.

For full details of eligibility, notice requirements, and procedures, please refer to the Loaded Dice Shared Parental Leave Policy.

## 10. Post-paternity leave

On returning from Paternity Leave, employees may request flexible working in line with the Loaded Dice Flexible Working Policy.

Requests will be considered in accordance with statutory requirements and business needs. Where possible, Loaded Dice will accommodate alternative arrangements such as part-time work, job-sharing, or phased returns.
